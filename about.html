What this is

Thirty-one apps that don't exist

Krypt LARP is a collection of interface studies — working mock-ups of banking, payments, trading and analytics screens for products that were invented for the purpose. Every number is editable, every screen animates, and all of it runs on your own machine.

How to use it

Four things worth knowing. The first one is the only one you have to remember, because everything else lives behind it.

The controls are hidden on purpose

There is no toolbar and no settings icon — a mock with a settings icon bolted to it stops looking like the thing it is imitating. Instead the whole control panel is behind one gesture, on every dashboard including ones you import. It differs by device, because a phone has no cursor to put a corner control under:

On a phone: tap three times anywhere on the screen.
On a desktop: click the small dot in the bottom-left corner.
What's in there Edit values · Reset · Currency · Home-screen icon · Demo watermark · Fullscreen · Your dashboards · back to the gallery

Every number is yours to change

Turn on Edit values and the editable fields become live text boxes — balances, names, transaction amounts, chart labels. What you type is saved to that dashboard on that device, so it's still there next time. Reset puts the whole screen back to how it shipped.

Where Open the panel → Edit values
Tap a highlighted number, type, tap away.
Open the panel → Reset to undo all of it.

It goes on your phone properly

Run the Wi-Fi server and scan the QR code, and the whole collection opens on your phone over your own network — nothing is published to the internet. From there any dashboard can be added to your Home Screen, where it launches full-screen with no browser chrome, like an installed app.

Where Desktop app → Phone, or run npm run serve
Then on the phone: Share → Add to Home Screen

The icon and name are yours too

Before you add one to your Home Screen you can give it any icon you like — a photo from your camera roll, or a plain lettered tile in one of eight colours — and any name up to 24 characters. It's stored per dashboard, on your device. Nothing is uploaded.

Where Open the panel → Home-screen icon
Set it before you add to Home Screen — that's the moment the phone reads it.

Make your own

The collection isn't fixed. Screenshot any interface you like, hand it to an AI with the prompt this app ships, and import what comes back — it edits, animates and installs exactly like the built-in ones.

  1. 1

    Screenshot something

    An app screen, a web dashboard, a concept off a portfolio site. A full-screen phone screenshot works best — the model needs the whole layout, not a crop of one card.

  2. 2

    Paste it into an AI chat with the prompt

    Any model that reads images will do. The prompt does the work: it specifies the file format, the phone frame, the edit mode and the naming rule, so what comes back behaves like a dashboard instead of being a flat picture in HTML.

  3. 3

    Save the reply as one .html file, and import it

    Drag it onto Your dashboards. It gets an icon, joins the collection, and shows up on your phone too when the Wi-Fi server is running.

What it isn't

Worth saying plainly, because a collection of editable balances is easy to misread and the name doesn't help.

These are not evidence of anything

They are studies in how a good interface is put together. They are not proof that you hold money, an asset, a position, a payout or an account. Presenting a mock as a real balance to get money, credit, investment or goodwill out of somebody is fraud in most places, and "it came from a demo app" is not a defence.

That is why every screen carries a small LARP · not a real app chip in the bottom-right corner, drawn inside the mock's own frame so a cropped screenshot keeps it. It is on by default. You can turn it off — open the panel → Demo watermark — and the app tells you in plain words what your screenshots will stop showing when you do. What you do with an unmarked screenshot is yours, and so is what follows.

Every company here is invented

Meridian is not a bank, Quill is not a payments app, Bodega doesn't sell anything. Names, logos, icons and palettes all belong to companies that do not exist — the same reason a set-design student builds a fake storefront.

Assets are the exception

BTC, ETH, SOL and USDC are real, with real artwork. A ticker names what an asset is, not who makes it — a wallet listing an invented token would be broken rather than careful. Stock tickers on invented P&L are renamed.

Nothing leaves your machine

Edits, icons and settings live in your browser's local storage on your own device. The Wi-Fi server serves to your own network only. There is no account and no upload.
